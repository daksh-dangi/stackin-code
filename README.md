# Stackin Code: Building on ML Research

Welcome to **Stackin Code**! This is the code repository containing the actual implementation for all the articles and projects I have posted on https://daksh-dangi.github.io/stackin-paper/ !

## Objectives

I am particularly excited by, and aim to focus on, methodologies for enabling continual learning and long-horizon memory management. I believe them to be fundamental limitations preventing the progression of LLMs beyond the current paradigm, and I want to spend my time there.

Much work is already underway towards these effects by the community, and I have been eager to contribute. My goal is to develop complete, end-to-end implementations that build on and refine the cutting edge research being published.

I aim to be selective & thorough in my work; extensively documenting both my failures and successes as I believe a vast portion of the learning process lies within the act of overcoming failure. I'd rather build 3 things I believe in, than 10 things just for the sake of it.

## AI Disclosure

I will try to be as transparent as possible regarding my usage of AI where relevant. While it can certainly be tempting to speed up progress and rely entirely on AI to implement these papers, my pursuit of imperfection leaves me seemingly orthogonal to this predisposition.

How can I learn, make mistakes, and be perfectly imperfect without touching upon the work myself ? The extent of my AI usage is to review & refine my conceptual understanding at the start of the project, and verify key implementation details at the end. I will do my best to point out if/where it's been used for other reasons in the articles.

Well, that and also this entire website was coded using AI. I am not a frontend developer :)

## Motivation

When tasked with explaining my motivation for this endeavor, I fall back to a quote from one of my favorite shows, Bleach, to offer some insight:

> "I dislike perfection. If something is perfect, there is nothing above it. There is no room for imagination. No space for intelligence or ability. [...] Be more wonderful than anything that existed before you, but never perfect."

The quote itself focuses on one aspect of perfection, namely that it forecloses growth. However, my lived experiences extend this meaning to one more aligned with my view. That is, even the *pursuit* of perfection forecloses growth. Chasing after a flawless first attempt stranded me at the starting line for the longest time; changing this mentality was one of the most defining moments of my college career.

Accepting failure as a byproduct of trying, and more importantly, building upon those failures has become my true goal - not the veneer of "perfection". This mindset change enabled me to complete my undergrad in 3 years and my MS in 1, and it is that very mindset that motivates me today to grow as a researcher.
